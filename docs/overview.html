Six findings that changed the design

Finding 01 · input

The number on the sign is the stop_id

stop_code == stop_id on all 5,118 stops. Unique, numeric, 3–5 digits, range 127–16549.

The SDK exposes no usable location API, so typing a stop number is the entire input UX — and it resolves in one integer lookup with no indirection table. The constraint and the data fit each other exactly.

Note: not uniformly five digits. A five-digit input mask would reject 57% of the system.

Finding 02 · the big one

The 207 KB TripUpdates feed carries 1,071 bytes of information

delay is constant across every stop of a trip — 127 of 127 trips have exactly one distinct value. And predicted == scheduled + delay held on 8,450 of 8,453 samples.

The whole feed reduces, without loss, to 153 records of {trip_id, delay, canceled}. Parse and discard: never store a StopTimeUpdate. A serialization library would materialise 9,536 objects to throw them away.

Finding 03 · scope

There is no realtime for MetroLink. None.

Zero vehicle positions, zero trip updates, for either line. Verified three ways across all 153 entities. On the snapshot's service day, 13 rail trips were in progress and not one had a live record.

Trains are shown from the timetable and always labelled scheduled. Presenting a timetable with the same weight as a live prediction would be a lie by typography.

Finding 04 · correctness

delay is signed, and a quarter of the values are negative

2,054 of 8,453 (24.3%) are negative — early buses are the ordinary case. A negative int32 arrives as a 10-byte sign-extended varint: -300 is d4fdffffffffffffff01, and those exact bytes appear 116 times in the fixture.

Read unsigned, -300 becomes 18,446,744,073,709,551,316 and the departure time lands about 584 billion years out. This is unit test #1.

Finding 05 · a silent data loss

One service_id exists only in calendar_dates.txt

319-T2 has no calendar.txt row at all, yet carries 250 rail trips and is active on exactly one date. There is also no feed_info.txt, so expiry has to be derived from max(calendar.end_date, calendar_dates.date).

A reader that builds its calendar from calendar.txt alone shows an empty rail schedule on Saturday 2026-08-08 and never says why.

Finding 06 · two days a year

The service day is noon minus twelve hours, not midnight

They coincide on 363 days. On 2026-03-08 the service day starts at 23:00 the evening before; on 2026-11-01 it starts at 01:00. Neither date is inside the current feed window, so the cases are unreachable from any snapshot taken now.

The first version of this very check got it wrong — Python does wall-clock arithmetic on an aware datetime, so subtracting 12 hours never crossed the offset. The port will meet the same landmine in java.time. Do the subtraction on an instant, and test with synthetic dates.

Also true, and worth knowing

Route numbers collideEight route_short_names are shared between Missouri and Illinois routes. "Route 1" is both Gold and Main Street – State Street. One agency row, so agency does not disambiguate.
Rail has four route IDs19731B/R and 19870B/R — one pair per pick. Rail route_id is not stable across picks; bus is. MLR/MLB are short names, not IDs.
16% of trips are doubled20 of 127 have every StopTimeUpdate repeated adjacently, byte-identical. Harmless under the one-delay-per-trip model, but evidence the producer is untidy.
Alerts have no effectNever set on any of 24. No detour/suspension classification is available — classify from the header text, or don't.
Vehicles carry lat/lon onlyNo stop_id, no current_stop_sequence, no bearing, no speed. "How far away" is a straight-line distance and must be labelled as one.
Bus coverage is good121 of 133 in-progress trips (91%), 48 of 50 running routes. Fix age p50 28 s, p90 61 s.
58% of stops flagged inaccessible2,978 of 5,118 carry wheelchair_boarding = 2. Real rider data that no other channel surfaces.
Gzip is worth 4.7×All three realtime feeds together: 235 KB raw, ~52 KB gzipped.

What the screen looks like

10624 GRAND METROLINK STATION ───────────────────────────────────── ○ 11:52 MLB MetroLink Blue Line BLUE LINE TO FAIRVIEW HEIGHTS scheduled ● 11:56 70 Grand MO NORTH — to Riverview 3 min late · live 22s ago ● 12:04 70 Grand MO canceled ───────────────────────────────────── Schedule 2026-07-30 · expires in 26 days

Live and scheduled are distinguished by weight and glyph, never colour — the tool is monochrome by discipline, since the panel is a colour OLED with no system grayscale filter. Route numbers are never shown bare. Canceled trips are shown, not removed: a rider who sees nothing assumes the app is broken.

Scope

  • Stop-number entry and up to 12 saved stops
  • Next 8 departures with early/late and cancellations
  • Trip detail — vehicle distance, every stop to the terminus
  • Browse — 38 rail stations, 45 transit centers, 62 routes
  • Service alerts, filtered to your stops' routes
  • Fares, payment, accessibility, contacts — bundled and dated
  • Other routes at this stop (530 stops serve more than one)
  • Data age everywhere; expiry replaces the list, not annotates it
  • Illinois routes — same feed, disambiguated MO/IL
  • Call-A-Ride and Via as reference cards
  • Destination / address search — needs a geocoder; that is a general network content client
  • "Stops near me" — no consumable location API; verified, not assumed
  • Trip planning — the first-party Directions tool already routes
  • Maps and route shapes — 3.5 MB of the 3.7 MB feed, and the one thing that reads browser-adjacent
  • Park-and-ride lots — not in the feed; the only source has no licence field
  • Departure alarms / push — SDK push docs incomplete, and a nudge is the wrong instinct
  • Timetable PDFs — no viewer, and the URL's pick ID can't be derived
  • Accounts, sync, history, sharing

Architecture, in one breath

Modulescore/ pure JVM, no Android imports, fully tested before any UI exists · data/ the only network and disk · ui/ renders, no logic
RT decoderHand-rolled, ~200 lines. Twelve fields to read, zero dependencies added, every trap becomes a test against real bytes. kotlinx-serialization kept live behind one interface — a probe is written and ready to settle it.
StorageBinary columnar index, not Room. 3.25 MB vs ~25 MB of SQLite; pure JVM so it lives in the test gate; byte-diffable against the Python reference. DataStore holds the twelve saved stop numbers and nothing else.
Timejava.time (kotlinx-datetime is not allow-listed). Service-day arithmetic on an Instant, with synthetic DST fixtures.
NetworkOkHttp. Conditional GETs, gzip, identifiable User-Agent, no background realtime polling ever. One daily job fetches a newer schedule. A 403 or 410 is a designed-for state — the licence is revocable and says so.
PermissionsINTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE. That is all. No location permission, though two are allow-listed.

Plan

Phase 0Verify, don't trustSix measurements before a line is written: publication cadence, conditional-request support, the protobuf probe, the SDK constraint sheet against a pinned commit, whether a tool can place a call, and the name — the tool id is permanent once published.
Phase 1The core, with no Android in sightTime, calendar, index, decoder, join. 22 tasks, all pure JVM. The gate: green tests and agreement with the Python oracle before any UI exists.
Phase 2Tool shelllighttool.toml, bundled assets, index loader with atomic swap, and the first pass through the plugin scan.
Phase 3ScreensSix of them. Pure wiring, because the logic is already proven. Includes a monochrome audit and a process-death test on every screen.
Phase 4Refresh and resilienceThe daily job, saved-stop survival across feed updates, the revocation path, airplane mode, and the two measurements that need a future feed.
Phase 5SubmissionREADME with the verbatim Terms of Use, the vetting defense, the finite-by-rule audit, the trademark sweep.
